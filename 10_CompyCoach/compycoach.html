CompyCoach

A fitness interruption app that lives in your menu bar, covers your screen, and gets harder the better you do.

Why this exists

The problem
with reminders

Most fitness reminder apps are polite. They send a notification you dismiss without reading. They're easy to ignore, so you ignore them.

CompyCoach is not polite. It covers your screen, tells you exactly what to do and how many reps, and won't go away until you log what you actually did. And if you keep hitting your targets, it raises them.

01
Interruption, not suggestion

It covers your entire screen, above every other window including the menu bar. You can force-quit in a genuine emergency. Otherwise, you're doing the squats.

02
Randomized, not clockwork

Fires roughly every hour with up to 15 minutes of random offset either way. Predictable timers get gamed. If you don't know exactly when it's coming, you can't plan around it.

03
Idle-aware

Before firing, it checks whether you've actually been at your desk. Mouse and keyboard untouched for 5+ minutes? It skips and reschedules. It won't interrupt an empty room.

04
Adaptive targets

Hitting your targets most days? The number goes up. Struggling? It backs off. Always slightly harder than comfortable. Never punishing, never coasting.

How it works

Four things
working together

I'd never opened Xcode before this. Here's how the pieces fit together.

The Clock
A background timer counts down to the next challenge. Before firing, it checks how long since you moved the mouse or hit a key. Idle 5+ minutes? It skips and reschedules. When you close the laptop and reopen it, a naive timer would fire immediately on wake. So on wake, the clock resets to a fresh interval from that moment.

The Log
Every confirmed challenge writes to a file on disk. Each entry gets added to the end rather than rewriting the whole file. If the app crashes mid-write, at most one entry is lost. Your history stays intact.

The Progression
After every challenge, the app looks back at the last 7 days for that exercise. Hitting your target most of the time? The number goes up. Consistently missing? It drops. Somewhere in between? It holds. Always slightly ahead of where you are.

Progression logic

The math

Targets adapt weekly based on your 7-day hit rate per exercise. The goal is a system that's always slightly ahead of where you are — not punishing, not coasting.

Core rule
Hit ≥80% → up 10% · Hit ≤50% → down 10% · Between → hold

Build notes

First time
in Xcode

I've never built a native Mac app. Most of what I learned had nothing to do with fitness.

The obvious approach didn't work

Menu bar apps are limited to a small dropdown by default. Getting a window above everything, including the menu bar itself, required a technique borrowed from how macOS handles screensavers. First instinct was wrong. Second instinct worked.

Laptops sleep

Close the lid, open it an hour later, and a naive timer would think no time had passed. The fix: listen for the Mac waking up and reset the clock from that moment. Obvious in hindsight.

Write carefully

Each log entry gets added to the end of the file, never rewriting the whole thing. If the app crashes mid-write, you lose at most one entry. The AI made this call without me asking. Right call.

Permissions are a maze

macOS has Full Disk Access, Files and Folders, and per-app toggles. "Claude" the app and "claude" the CLI are different entries that each need their own access. Stuck here longer than I'd like to admit.

AI as build partner

Claude Code wrote every line of Swift. I planned the architecture, reviewed each task, and made decisions at checkpoints. I still understand what was built and why. That feels like the right way to work in a language you don't know yet.

It actually works

Running daily since I shipped it. Squats are already up from 10 to 11. The interruption is genuinely annoying in the right way. Turns out building the tool yourself makes you more likely to use it.
